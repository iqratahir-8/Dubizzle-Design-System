# Egypt realism cues — for prompts and for review

Concrete nouns beat adjectives. Pick 5–12 of these for the SETTING slot.

## Streets and traffic
- **Right-hand traffic, left-hand-drive cars.**
- Fleet: compact sedans and small crossovers (prompt "generic compact sedan / small crossover",
  debadged, slightly dusty) — never a named make or model. Context: microbuses, tuk-tuks in side
  streets, white taxis.
- **Plates:** Egyptian plates carry Arabic letters and digits under an "EGYPT / مصر" band (light blue
  for private cars). Models produce fake glyphs — **angle plates away or ask for a plain blank plate.**

## City architecture (Cairo, Giza, Alexandria)
- 5–12-storey sand, beige or ochre concrete blocks; exposed brick on unfinished floors
- Balconies with split AC units, satellite dishes, laundry lines
- Shopfront awnings (no readable signs), kerbside parking, dust, light haze

## Compounds (New Cairo, Sheikh Zayed, 6th of October)
- Beige stucco villas and townhouses, palms, paved walkways, low hedges, guard booths, gated
  entrances — **no developer signage or names**

## Interiors
- Tiled or ceramic floors, heavy curtains, ornate gilded furniture beside modern pieces,
  ceiling fans or split AC units, real clutter

## Light
- Midday: strong high sun, hard short shadows
- Late afternoon: warm low sun, dusty haze
- Avoid lush green northern-European light and grey drizzle

## People
- Modest everyday clothing; women with and without hijab is realistic for Egypt; men in trousers and
  shirts, sometimes a galabeya in a local context
- **Never Gulf dress (thobe, ghutra) as "Arab" shorthand**
- Families often multi-generational
- Ramadan creative: higher modesty, family and evening light (lanterns without text)
- No alcohol; no beachwear in general campaigns
- Default: no identifiable faces (backs, three-quarter-away, distance)

## Phones and objects
- Generic dark phone slabs; dubizzle UI composited onto screens afterwards
- Keys, papers and cash: no readable text or denominations

## Clichés to avoid
- Pyramids, camels, desert, felucca at sunset for everyday commerce — dubizzle's Egypt is urban and
  suburban, everyday, lived-in

## Never in the pixels
- Prices (EGP), headlines, CTAs, Arabic or English copy — HTML overlay only
